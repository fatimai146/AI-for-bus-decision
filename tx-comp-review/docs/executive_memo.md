# Executive Memo: Texas State Workforce Compensation Review

**To:** State HR and Compensation leadership
**From:** Iman Fatima, Compensation Analytics
**Data:** Texas state payroll as of October 1, 2026 (139,656 regular full-time classified employees, $9.36B base pay); FY2026–27 salary schedules; BLS wage data, May 2025

---

## Bottom line

The state does **not pay for experience within a job**: ten or more years in
the same job at the same agency is worth about 5% in pay. Pay growth comes
almost entirely from promotion. In 7 job titles, new hires are now paid
significantly more than 10-year veterans. A pay floor for experienced staff
costs **$35M a year (0.4% of payroll)**. Professional and technology base pay
sits roughly **14–16% below the Texas market median** (before benefits). Pay
equity within jobs is strong; the remaining gaps come from which jobs people hold.

## What we found

**1. Experience is barely paid within a job.**
Compared with a new hire (<2 years) in the same job title at the same agency,
an employee with 10–20 years earns just 5.3% more, and one with 20+ years 6.7%
more. Across a whole job series (counting promotions) the premium is 15–19%.

**2. Most job titles give long service little or nothing.**
We tested 154 job titles with at least 20 new hires and 20 staff with 10+
years, comparing people inside the same agency. In 72% of them, 10+ year staff
earn less than 5% more than new hires, or no more at all (median premium 3.3%).
In 7 titles, new hires are paid significantly more:

| Job title | Agencies | 10+ yr staff vs new hires, same agency | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nurse III | 6 | −10.0% | −12.9% to −7.0% |
| Investigator V | 17 | −8.3% | −12.7% to −3.5% |
| IT Business Analyst III | 26 | −7.8% | −13.3% to −2.0% |
| Contract Admin Manager I | 13 | −6.8% | −12.2% to −1.0% |
| Contract Specialist III | 18 | −5.8% | −10.6% to −0.8% |
| Safety Officer I | 3 | −5.7% | −8.8% to −2.4% |
| Program Specialist VII | 31 | −3.2% | −6.2% to −0.2% |

**3. Range position tells the same story.**
New hires sit 47% of the way into their pay range (compa-ratio 0.99).
Staff with 10–20 years sit at only 59%, and 20+ years at 62%.

**4. The salary structure has room the State does not use.**
Ranges are wide (median spread 63%). Career-ladder levels step up a median
13.7% in midpoint, within the common 10–15% guide, but adjacent levels overlap
by 79% (guide: under 50%). Despite that overlap, only 6% of staff are paid
above the next level's median at their agency, because pay sits mid-range.
The structure could reward experience; the pay policy does not move people
through it.

**5. Pay equity within jobs is strong.**
Raw gaps are sizeable: women earn 6.3% less than men, and Black and Hispanic
employees 14.4% and 11.0% less than White employees. Once job title, agency
and tenure are matched, every gap is under 0.5%. Three agencies show a
statistically significant adjusted gender gap, all under 1%: the Office of the
Attorney General (−1.0%), TCEQ (−0.8%), and Parks and Wildlife (−0.5%).

**6. Professional and technology pay trails the market.**
Across 21 benchmark jobs, state median pay is 86% of the Texas market median
(technology roles: 84%). The largest gaps: systems administrators (0.71),
accountants (0.74), cybersecurity analysts (0.77), attorneys (0.79).

## Cost of a pay floor for experienced staff

| Option | Employees raised | Annual cost | % of payroll | Median raise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. No 5+ year employee below the new-hire median of their job and agency | 6,420 | $35.3M | 0.38% | $3,045 (4.6%) |
| B. Option A plus 1% per year of service (cap 10%) | 38,808 | $180.8M | 1.93% | $3,380 (5.4%) |

No raise takes anyone above their range maximum. Costs are base pay only.
Health and Human Services Commission ($19.0M) and TxDOT ($7.3M) account for
75% of Option A. These price a policy choice; some of the gap below any
median is normal pay spread rather than underpayment.

## Recommendations

1. **Review the 7 titles where new hires are paid more** (Nurse III,
   Investigator V and IT Business Analyst III first) and correct tenured pay.
2. **Consider Option A** as a statewide floor in the next budget cycle.
3. **Adopt an in-range progression policy** (Option B or a cheaper version) so
   experience is paid without requiring a promotion. The existing range width
   already allows this. Review the 19% of ladder steps under 10%, where a
   promotion barely changes pay.
4. **Run a proper market study** for systems administrators, cybersecurity
   analysts, accountants and data roles, where the base-pay gap looks largest,
   using level-matched survey data and total rewards.
5. **Review the three agencies** with significant adjusted gender gaps at the
   job-title level.
6. **Track representation, not just pay:** raw gaps come from job placement,
   so promotion and hiring pipelines are the lever for equity.

## Caveats

- The payroll gives a hire date, not the date someone entered their current
  title. A check using entry-level titles only (where nobody can have been
  promoted in) gives the same result.
- Tenure is not total experience: some new hires bring prior experience.
- The market comparison uses base pay only, pools job levels, and matches
  occupations approximately. State benefits are typically richer, so it
  overstates the total-rewards gap.
