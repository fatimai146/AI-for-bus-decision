# Executive Memo: Texas State Workforce Compensation Review

**To:** State HR and Compensation leadership
**From:** Iman Fatima, Compensation Analytics
**Data:** Texas state payroll as of October 1, 2026 (139,656 regular full-time classified employees, $9.36B base pay); FY2026–27 salary schedules; BLS wage data, May 2025

---

## Bottom line

The state pays new hires competitively for its ranges, but it does **not pay for
experience within a job**. Pay growth comes almost entirely from promotion. As a
result, new hires now out-earn 10-year veterans in 4 of every 10 job groups.
Fixing the worst of this costs **$35M a year (0.4% of payroll)**. Separately,
professional and technology roles sit **14–16% below the Texas market**. Pay
equity within jobs is strong; the remaining gaps come from which jobs people hold.

## What we found

**1. Experience is barely paid within a job.**
Compared with a new hire (<2 years) in the same job title at the same agency,
an employee with 10–20 years earns just 5.3% more, and one with 20+ years 6.7%
more. Across a whole job series (counting promotions) the premium is 15–19%.

**2. Compression and inversion are widespread.**
Of 353 agency × job-title groups large enough to compare, 69% are compressed
(new-hire median at 95% or more of the 10+ year median) and 41% are inverted
(new hires paid more). Clear examples statewide:

| Job title | New-hire median | 10+ year median | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human Resources Specialist IV | $66,075 | $58,904 | +12.2% |
| Nurse III | $89,691 | $81,176 | +10.5% |
| Investigator VI | $82,425 | $75,442 | +9.3% |
| IT Support Specialist IV | $66,000 | $61,377 | +7.5% |
| Assistant Attorney General V | $145,000 | $135,723 | +6.8% |

**3. Range position tells the same story.**
New hires sit at the 47th percentile of their range (compa-ratio 0.99).
Staff with 10–20 years sit at only the 59th, and 20+ years at the 62nd.

**4. Pay equity within jobs is strong.**
Raw gaps are sizeable: women earn 6.3% less than men, and Black and Hispanic
employees 14.4% and 11.0% less than White employees. Once job title, agency
and tenure are matched, every gap is under 0.5%. Three agencies show a
statistically significant adjusted gender gap, all under 1%: the Office of the
Attorney General (−1.0%), TCEQ (−0.8%), and Parks and Wildlife (−0.5%).

**5. Professional and technology pay trails the market.**
Across 21 benchmark jobs, state median pay is 86% of the Texas market median
(technology roles: 84%). The largest gaps: systems administrators (0.71),
accountants (0.74), cybersecurity analysts (0.77), attorneys (0.79).

## Cost to fix compression

| Option | Employees raised | Annual cost | % of payroll | Median raise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Compression floor: lift 5+ year staff to the new-hire median of their job and agency | 6,420 | $35.3M | 0.38% | $3,045 (4.6%) |
| B. Floor plus 1% per year of service (cap 10%) | 38,808 | $180.8M | 1.93% | $3,380 (5.4%) |

No raise takes anyone above their range maximum. Costs are base pay only.
Health and Human Services Commission ($19.0M) and TxDOT ($7.3M) account for
75% of Option A.

## Recommendations

1. **Fund Option A** in the next budget cycle, starting with inverted titles in
   HHSC, TxDOT and the Attorney General's office.
2. **Adopt an in-range progression policy** (Option B or a cheaper version) so
   experience is paid without requiring a promotion.
3. **Make targeted market adjustments** for systems administrators,
   cybersecurity analysts, accountants and data roles, where the gap to market
   is 20–35%.
4. **Review the three agencies** with significant adjusted gender gaps at the
   job-title level.
5. **Track representation, not just pay:** raw gaps come from job placement,
   so promotion and hiring pipelines are the lever for equity.

## Caveats

The payroll gives the agency hire date, not the date someone entered their
current title. A check using entry-level titles only (where nobody can have
been promoted in) gives the same result. Market matches are approximate,
especially for data roles.
