## Phase 2: Pipeline Building (Weeks 1-4)

### Volume targets

- **10-15 tailored applications per week** across role families
- **3-5 warm outreach messages per week** (alumni, referrals, LinkedIn connections at target companies)
- Track everything in a simple spreadsheet: Company | Role | Date Applied | Source | Status | Follow-up Date

### Where to find roles

| Source | Why |
|--------|-----|
| **Purdue Handshake / Career Center** | Employers already recruiting from Purdue; warm leads |
| **LinkedIn Jobs** (set alerts) | Broadest funnel; use "Entry Level" + "New Grad" filters |
| **Company career pages** (direct) | For target companies, apply direct AND through LinkedIn |
| **Levels.fyi / Glassdoor** | Comp benchmarking and company research |
| **Referrals** | Ask Cloudflare colleagues, Purdue alumni, Nestle contacts |
| **Purdue BAIM alumni Slack/Discord** | Alumni from your exact program, most likely to help |

### Target company tiers

**Tier 1 — High-priority (strong analytics culture, likely to sponsor)**

**Tier 2 — Strong analytics orgs**

**Tier 3 — HR/People Analytics specific**

### The return offer play

Your strongest lead is Cloudflare itself. If you haven't already:
1. Ask your manager directly: "Is there a path to a return offer?"
2. Ask if they can refer you to other teams if your team doesn't have headcount
3. Get LinkedIn recommendations from your manager and skip-level before the internship memory fades

## Phase 3: Interview Prep (Ongoing, Weeks 2-8)

### For analytics / BI / product roles

| Area | What to prep | Resources |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **SQL** | Window functions, CTEs, self-joins, aggregation edge cases | LeetCode SQL, StrataScratch, DataLemur |
| **Statistics** | A/B testing, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, p-values, power | Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments (book) |
| **Product sense** | Metric definition, root cause analysis, trade-off frameworks | Decode and Conquer, Cracking the PM Interview |
| **Case studies** | "How would you measure X?" | Practice with peers; your Cloudflare QoH project IS a case study |
| **Behavioral** | STAR format stories from Cloudflare + Nestle + Purdue | Write 8-10 stories covering leadership, conflict, ambiguity, impact |

### For data engineering / ML roles

| Area | What to prep |
|------|-------------|
| **Python** | Pandas, data structures, basic algorithms |
| **System design (data)** | Data warehouse design, ETL pipelines, batch vs streaming |
| **ML fundamentals** | Bias-variance, cross-validation, feature engineering, model selection |
| **Your Cloudflare project** | Walk through the pipeline architecture, modeling decisions, and why you chose ranking over point prediction |

### For HR / People Analytics roles

These interviews lean heavier on:
- Stakeholder management stories (you have strong ones from Nestle + Cloudflare)
- Privacy/ethics thinking (your fairness audit recommendation is gold)
- Change management (pilot rollout, feedback tracker)
- Less leetcode, more "tell me about a time you had to convince a non-technical leader"

### Your killer talking points (memorize these)

1. **"I built Cloudflare's first Quality-of-Hire metric"** — shows you can define ambiguous problems, not just solve assigned ones
2. **"I caught a data leakage bug that inflated model performance"** — shows intellectual honesty and technical rigor
3. **"I shipped a live tool, not just an analysis"** — shows you go from insight to product
4. **"I positioned the model for ranking, not prediction, because that's what the data could support"** — shows mature judgment about what models can and can't do
5. **"I recommended a fairness audit before operational use"** — shows responsible AI thinking

## Phase 4: Closing (Weeks 6-12)

### Negotiation prep

- Research comp bands on Levels.fyi for each role/company
- For new grad roles, there's usually less room to negotiate base, but signing bonus and equity are more flexible
- If you get multiple offers, use them as leverage (ethically and transparently)

### Decision framework

When comparing offers, weight these factors:
1. **Team and manager quality** (biggest predictor of early-career growth)
2. **Learning trajectory** (will you be doing real analytics or pulling reports?)
3. **Sponsorship / visa path** (if applicable)
4. **Compensation** (total comp, not just base)
5. **Brand on resume** (matters more for your first full-time role)
